Design review, 5 dimensions. Reviewed 16 Nov 2026 against the running build at 1280 x 800 and 375 x 812.

Krish Iyer portfolio: from blueprint to build

Verdict: the concept holds from the first frame to the footer and the flagship moment is real, not decorative. The weak spots were in execution details and two functional bugs, both found by looking at the render and both fixed. What is left is mostly content the owner has to supply.

The five dimensions

1. Philosophy consistency

8/ 10, strong

One idea runs through everything: Prussian-blue ground, cyan line work, tungsten for anything lit. The hero grid, the .sheet corner ticks, the title block in the nav, the line-drawing project covers, and the cyanotype portrait all argue the same thesis. Drift: the Off the clock panels read as a card grid first and a drawing set second, and the white polaroids are the one object that ignores the palette.

2. Visual hierarchy

7/ 10, strong

Each section has a clear first read. Two places compete. PROJECTS (h2, up to 7.5rem) is followed within one screen by ARCHITECH at up to 15rem, so the section title is immediately outranked. In Experience the tungsten metrics at 5.75rem are louder than the role titles above them. That is intended for a 60-second scan, but it means the eye lands on 71.9% before it knows what the role is.

3. Detail execution

7/ 10, strong (was 6)

Fixed in this pass: chart value labels sat on top of the next row's label (row height 62 to 76); the cyanotype tint was a 9px square because .sheet::after and .cyanotype::after collided; the fixed title block covered card text at the bottom right; the hero's role note ran through the wireframe. Remaining: the 400px headshot is soft at 350 CSS px on a 2x screen, the climbing holds are small at phone width, and the globe pins say "Example".

4. Functionality

8/ 10, strong (was 6)

Two real bugs were caught in the browser. The shuttle animation passed path data where MotionPath expects an element, threw on every tick, and stalled every later scroll animation. A screen-reader-only table ignored its 1px box and made phones lay the page out at 581px. Both are fixed. Nav, keyboard access, project panel focus trap, lightbox keys, no-WebGL fallback and reduced-motion branches are in place. Not verified: the reduced-motion path on screen, a physical phone, and a Lighthouse run.

5. Innovation

8/ 10, strong

The viewer is the memorable move: a live procedural building whose roof morphs between flat, gable and hip, with a section cut and a fire route computed by an actual Dijkstra search that falls back to a window when the door is blocked. It demonstrates the project instead of describing it. Supporting moves that earn their place: the name filling from the ground up, line-drawing covers generated from the same geometry as the 3D model, and the ordered-dither preloader exit.

Actions

Keep

  • The two-accent rule. Cyan is drawn, tungsten is lit. Do not add a third.
  • The viewer controls and its plain-language status line.
  • Line drawings as covers. They are honest about missing screenshots.
  • One pinned scene. Do not pin anything else.
  • All copy in content.ts.

Fix

  • Supply real screenshots for Make-A-Dash, Motion Mentor and FuturePop, which still use illustrations. (Done since the review for ArchiTech, Ai-FixIT and VeinViz.)
  • Re-export the screenshots larger. 806 px wide is soft at full size.
  • Use a larger headshot (1600 x 2000).
  • Swap the resume PDF for one without a phone number, if that rule still stands.
  • Run Lighthouse on the deployed build and test on a real phone.
  • Check reduced motion on a device with the setting on.
